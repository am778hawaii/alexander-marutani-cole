# Executive Decision Memo

**To:** Chief Financial Officer
**From:** Alexander Marutani-Cole
**Date:** July 31, 2026
**Subject:** FX Hedge Framing – EUR 4,500,000 Receivable

Our firm expects to receive **EUR 4,500,000 in one year**, creating a foreign exchange exposure because the euro amount is fixed while the U.S. dollar value is uncertain. If the EUR/USD exchange rate is **1.10** at settlement, the receivable will be worth approximately **$4,950,000**. If the exchange rate declines to **1.00**, the same receivable will be worth only **$4,500,000**, creating a **$450,000 reduction in expected cash flow**. A currency movement of this size is well within the range of normal annual market fluctuations and could materially affect budgeting, profitability, and cash-flow planning.

Three primary hedging strategies should be considered. A **forward contract** would lock in a future exchange rate today, providing certainty about the dollar proceeds and making budgeting more reliable. The trade-off is that if the euro appreciates, the company cannot benefit from the more favorable exchange rate. A **money market hedge** uses borrowing and lending to create a synthetic forward position. This approach also provides certainty and may be attractive when financing conditions are favorable, but it requires additional transactions and depends on current interest rates in both currencies. Finally, a **currency option** provides the right, but not the obligation, to exchange euros at a predetermined rate. This protects the company against adverse exchange-rate movements while allowing participation if the euro strengthens. The primary disadvantage is the option premium, which increases the overall cost of the hedge.

I recommend proceeding with the structured evaluation planned for the remaining project phases before selecting a final strategy. In **Phase 2**, I will document the hedge specifications and assumptions. In **Phase 3**, I will use AI to construct the financial models while independently auditing every calculation and assumption. **Phase 4** will incorporate current market data, including spot rates, forward rates, and interest rates, to produce realistic hedge valuations. Finally, **Phase 5** will validate the results through sensitivity analysis, compare the three hedge alternatives, and present a recommendation supported by quantitative evidence. This phased approach will allow management to select the hedge that best balances cost, risk reduction, and financial flexibility.
